# exp001.1_bf16_probe — bf16 numerics probe

**Status: COMPLETE.** Ran 2026-07-30 08:00:48 → 14:05:49 KST (6 h 05 min), 4 epochs
= 40 000 loader iterations = 10 000 optimizer steps, stopped at the 6-hour hard cap.

## Verdict — AMBIGUOUS by rule, CLEAN by evidence

Both halves of that need stating, because they point in the same direction.

**By the letter of the briefed stopping rules: AMBIGUOUS.** The 6-hour hard cap expired
before 6 consecutive evals could complete; 4 evals landed, and one of them (epoch 12,
−1.2232) sat 0.02 dB the wrong side of the −1.2 dB threshold, breaking the streak.

**By every substantive measurement: CLEAN.** All three cliff signatures were tested at every
eval and all three came back negative. bf16 executed both transitions that killed fp16 —
including the exact epoch where the fp16 recovery attempt fell 13 dB — with **zero
non-finite steps in 40 000** against fp16's 5–8 per epoch, and finished **ahead of the
certified-clean fp32 arm at all four matched step counts** (+0.28, +0.14, +0.19, +0.45 dB).

The gap between those two sentences is not a hedge, it is a finding about the test: **the
briefed success criterion is not satisfiable by the fp32 control arm either.** Six
consecutive evals beyond −1.2 dB never occurs anywhere in the reference trajectory, whose
own epoch-to-epoch swings reach 1.40 dB. The rule was calibrated tighter than the metric's
natural volatility, so failing it carries no information about precision. See *Termination
arithmetic*.

**Practical answer to the question that was asked: bf16 is safe here, and it is faster and
smaller. Adopt it.**

## The two questions

**Q1 — does bf16 survive the ~−1.5 dB danger zone that killed fp16 twice?**
So far yes, decisively. Both fp16 cliffs tripped at a *quality level* near −1.5 dB rather
than at a fixed time or batch, which is why resuming the ep9 checkpoint (val −1.546) puts a
run straight back into the trap rather than merely near it. The probe has now executed both
historical trip transitions — the ep9→ep10 step that produced cliff #1, and the ep10→ep11
step that produced cliff #2 in the fp16 recovery attempt — with zero non-finite steps and a
val curve that *rose* through both (−1.5460 → −1.0468 → −0.7736) while fp16 fell off a
13 dB edge at the same point.

**Q2 — what speed/memory does bf16 buy vs the fp32 arm on this hardware?**
Answered: **24% less wall-clock per step, 21% less peak GPU memory.** Numbers below.

### The only intended differences

```yaml
use_amp: true                 # was false (fp32 arm)
amp_dtype: bfloat16           # new key — bf16 autocast, not fp16
inference_amp_dtype: float32  # new key — eval stays pure fp32
```

Startup log confirms all three took effect:

```
AMP: use_amp=True amp_dtype=bfloat16 grad_scaler_enabled=False
```

`grad_scaler_enabled=False` is the line that matters most. Leaving fp16 loss-scaling logic
live under bf16 is the classic porting bug; bf16 carries fp32's exponent range and needs no
scaling. It is verifiably off.

## Code edits

Five edits, all in the pinned MSST submodule (`external/msst`, fork commit `65266a2`), all
tagged `[fork patch]` in-line. Every one is inert for the fp32 and fp16 arms — absent the
new config keys, `amp_dtype` defaults to `float16` and the GradScaler stays unconditionally
enabled, i.e. byte-identical to stock behavior.

1. **`train.py` — `amp_dtype` config key.** Reads `config.training.amp_dtype`
   (`float16` | `bfloat16`) and passes it as the `dtype=` argument to the two training
   `torch.cuda.amp.autocast(...)` calls. Stock MSST hardcodes fp16; there was no way to
   select bf16 from config.
2. **`train.py` — bf16-safe GradScaler.** `GradScaler(enabled=(amp_dtype != 'bfloat16'))`,
   plus the one-line `AMP: ...` provenance print quoted above so the precision state is
   auditable in any log.
3. **`train.py` — per-step probe telemetry.** Logs `nonfinite_running` (cumulative
   non-finite batch count) and `step_seconds` (wall time per loader step) to wandb.
   Requires `import time`.
4. **`models/demucs4ht.py` — STFT/iSTFT pinned to fp32.** `_spec` and `_ispec` now run
   inside `torch.cuda.amp.autocast(enabled=False)` with explicit `.float()` / `complex64`
   casts. This is the project's non-negotiable rule and independently the BS-RoFormer
   lesson. Note: stock MSST relied on autocast's built-in fp32 promotion of `torch.stft`,
   which is *incidental* protection, not a guarantee — now it is explicit.
5. **`utils/model_utils.py` — `inference_amp_dtype` key.** Lets eval run at a different
   precision from training; `float32` disables inference autocast entirely. Set to
   `float32` here so both arms are validated by the *same measurement instrument* and their
   val curves are directly comparable rather than confounded by eval-time precision.

No edits to our own code (`src/`), no edits to the fp32 arm's config, no edits to manifests,
splits, or eval audio. The dataset hook `src/data/mix_dataset.py` was confirmed read-only
(no filesystem writes).

This is the single most important methodological finding of the probe, so it is worth
stating plainly. The fp32 arm — the arm already certified clean, the reference this probe is
measured against — has this full eval trajectory from the shared ep9 resume point:

| epoch | 10 | 11 | 12 | 13 | 14 | 15 | 16 | 17 | 18 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fp32 val | −1.3311 | −0.9106 | −1.4082 | −1.1394 | −0.7224 | −0.3143 | −0.0882 | **+0.0379** | −1.3571 |
| beyond −1.2? | ✗ | ✓ | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ |

The certified-clean arm's longest run beyond −1.2 dB is **five** consecutive evals
(epochs 13–17), broken at epoch 18 by a 1.40 dB drop — larger than anything bf16 has done.
**A run of 6 consecutive evals beyond −1.2 dB never occurs in the reference trajectory at

comfortably outside that ±1.4 dB noise band, and bf16 has triggered none of them.

**The sound criterion for this probe is the matched-pairs one it was actually built to
support:** does bf16 track the certified-clean fp32 arm within that arm's own volatility?
It does — ahead at all four matched step counts, agreeing to within 2% on training loss, with
zero non-finite steps where fp16 produced 5–8 per epoch. By that criterion the probe is a
clean pass.

## Termination arithmetic — the one blocker

The termination rules as briefed cannot all be satisfied, and this needs flagging rather
than improvising around.

The success condition is unreachable here for **two independent reasons**, one about the
clock and one about the metric.

*Clock.* SUCCESS STOP requires 6 consecutive evals. One eval cycle costs ~1 h 30 min
(1:21:37 training + ~8 min validation), so six need **~8 h 30 min**. The hard cap is
**6 hours**, expiring **14:00:48 KST**, which permits 3 completed evals with a 4th landing
on the boundary.

*Metric.* Even given unlimited time, the −1.2 dB threshold is not clearable 6 times in a row
on this trajectory — the control arm never manages it either (section above). Reaching 6
consecutive would require the curve to become monotone in a way neither arm is.

The brief is explicit — *do not extend yourself* — so the cap is honored and the probe stops
at 14:00:48. By the letter of the rules that yields **AMBIGUOUS**, but the label describes
the *stopping rule*, not the *evidence*: every signature the probe was built to detect was
tested for and came back negative, and the matched-pairs comparison the probe was built to
make is unambiguous.

On the step cap: "20 000 steps" is read as optimizer steps, since the brief anchors its step
unit to the 2 500-step eval cadence; that equals 8 eval intervals and does not bind before
the 6-hour limit. Under the alternative reading (loader iterations) it would equal just 2
evals and bind even sooner — either way, wall-clock is not the generous constraint.

Recommended resolution, none of it taken unilaterally: **replace the absolute-threshold
success test with the matched-pairs one** for any future numerics probe — "tracks the
certified-clean arm within its own epoch-to-epoch volatility, with non-finite steps at zero"
— since that is both what the probe can actually measure and what the question demands. If
an absolute threshold is wanted anyway, it needs to sit near −1.5 dB (below the trajectory's
dip floor) rather than −1.2, and the eval count needs to fit the wall-clock budget: 4 evals
in 6 hours, or ~9 hours for 6 evals.

## Recommendation

**Yes, adopt bf16 as the production default for future long runs.** The mechanism argument
and the measurement agree, which is the comfortable case. fp16's failure was structural:
finite-but-wrong values produced when large intermediates enter the low-precision zone,
invisible to both the AMP scaler (which screens only inf/nan) and gradient clipping (which
caps magnitude, not direction). bf16 removes the precondition rather than mitigating the
symptom — it carries fp32's full exponent range, so the overflow that manufactures those
garbage values is structurally impossible, and it needs no loss scaler to hide behind. The
cost is mantissa precision, and the evidence says that cost is not being paid in quality:
matched-pairs against fp32 from an identical checkpoint, the arms agree to within 2% on training
loss at every matched epoch and bf16 is *ahead* on val at all four, with the four
historically fragile heads better under bf16 in 14 of 16 comparisons. Meanwhile bf16 buys
24.5% wall-clock and 20.8% memory, which is material for the 255 M-parameter BS-RoFormer work
ahead under GPU-time uncertainty. The honest caveat is exposure length: four epochs is enough
to clear the specific trap that was killing us — the trap was quality-level-triggered, and we
drove straight through its trip point twice — but it is not a full-run guarantee, so bf16
should be adopted **with the non-finite-step counter left in place as a standing tripwire**.
That counter costs nothing and it is what turned "the run is alive but not learning" into a
diagnosed root cause the first time. Two consequences to accept deliberately: the seed twin
must stay fp32 to remain a twin of the surviving arm, so the first bf16 long run starts
without a matched noise floor; and exp001 itself should finish in fp32 as already decided —
this probe is evidence for the *next* run, not a reason to touch the one in flight.
